**Overview:-**
  This project involves the modelling of a hammer mill, a commonly used machine for
  crushing, grinding, and size reduction of various materials. Typical applications include grain
  processing, biomass crushing, and material preparation for industrial processes.\
  The complete assembly contains the major functional units found in real hammer mills:
  • Feeding Hopper – allows controlled input of raw material.
  • Hammering/Crushing Chamber – the central region where material is broken down.
  • Rotor Housing – contains the rotating hammer assembly (represented in simplified
  form).
  • Supporting Frame – provides structural stability.
  • Cyclone Separator – separates heavier particles from air flow after grinding.
  The model represents the essential external geometry, flow path, and arrangement of a
  working hammer mill system. It demonstrates key mechanical concepts such as material
  inlet guidance, impact-based crushing, airflow-assisted discharge, and particulate separation
  using a cyclone.

**Part Modelling:-**
  Each component was modelled individually in SOLIDWORKS using standard CAD tools such
  as extrude, revolve, sweep, shell, cut-extrude, and fillet features.
  The main parts created include:
  • Hopper
  • Main mill housing
  • Stand structure
  • Circular inlet and outlet ducts
  • Cyclone separator body, cone, and lid
  • Support pipes and connectors
  Dimensions were chosen to maintain realistic proportions of a medium-size hammer mill.
  Material appearance was assigned for visual clarity (steel, sheet metal, and plastic
  components), though exact mechanical material definitions were not required for this stage.

**Assembly Modelling:-**
  After creating all individual parts, the complete hammer mill system was assembled using
  coincident, concentric, distance, and parallel mates wherever needed.
  Key relationships included:
  • Aligning the hopper with the upper opening of the mill chamber
  • Positioning the discharge duct with the cyclone inlet
  • Fixing the cyclone vertically with a proper supporting connection
  • Ensuring the frame supports the housing without interference
  The final assembly clearly demonstrates how material would flow from the hopper into the
  mill, pass through the crushing region, and finally move through the ducting into the cyclone
  separator.
